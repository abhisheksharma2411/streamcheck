# Peer review — StreamCheck

Adversarial review run 2026-09-29, after a clean-room re-run regenerated every
result from one revision. Numbers re-derived from `results/*.json` directly
rather than from the prose or from `scripts/verify_claims.py`.

**Recommendation: accept with minor revision.** The central construct — the
replay horizon assembled from four artifacts — is well motivated and the
execution arm genuinely corrected the authors' own rules, which is the part of
the paper that carries the most weight. One stronger result was sitting in the
data unstated and is now stated.

---

## What this review changed

### 1. A sharper result was available and left implicit

`artifacts_needed_histogram` is `{1: 245, 2: 60, 3: 210}` and
`duration_artifacts_needed_histogram` is `{1: 65, 2: 30, 3: 210}`. The paper
reported both lines adjacently without drawing the inference. Duration findings
are a subset of all findings, so equal counts in the three-artifact bucket mean
the two sets *coincide*: **every finding that needs three artifacts is a
duration finding.** Needing to read across the configuration is not a general
property of static analysis on this corpus — it is specifically what the replay
horizon costs. That is the paper's own thesis, and the data states it more
strongly than the prose did. Now in the body with a claim asserting it.

### 2. Wall-clock figures were quoted to a precision they do not hold

The tightest observed gap over the scaled key lifetime moved from 30.6 s to
30.5 s between runs that changed no code, and the live table's per-rule gaps
moved with it (SC101 30.6–48.0 → 30.5–47.9, SC002 5.9 → 6.2). The argument
rests on the margin being under a second, not on its exact tenth, so the claim
now asserts `0 < margin < 1 s` alongside the reported figure, and the prose
says the number is wall clock and moves. Machine timings do not reproduce and
should not be argued from at full precision.

### 3. Results spanned two revisions and nothing checked

`check_provenance.py` and `check_prose_numbers.py` were in `scripts/` and
absent from the Makefile, so `make reproduce` finished green while
`e3_live.json` came from one revision and the other eight from another — and
the committed `e3_live.json` had itself been produced from a dirty tree
(`50e7259-dirty`). Both gates are now in `make verify` and in `reproduce`, and
all nine results come from `d2748f7`.

### 4. Literal markdown in the published PDF

Three contribution headings were written `**like this**` and rendered with the
asterisks visible. Every gate passed while the document was visibly wrong,
because none of them reads sentences.

SC106 is the finding I would lead with. The cap is **measured on a live broker**
(`apache/kafka 3.9.0 KRaft`), the refusal is probed rather than assumed, and it
matches the constant the checker carries. A recovery assumption above roughly
fourteen minutes therefore has no application-level fix at all — not a tuning
problem, an impossibility.

## Minor

**The 14-minute threshold is configuration-dependent and the abstract rounds
it.** `e7.sc106_threshold_minutes` ranges 9.83 to 14.67 across checkpoint
settings. The abstract says "above a 14-minute recovery assumption", which is
the loosest of the four. Worth one clause noting the threshold falls to under
ten minutes at a 300-second checkpoint interval — that makes the finding worse,
not better, so there is no reason to state only the mildest case.

## Scope, correctly stated by the paper

The corpus is generated by the authors, including boundary cases one second
either side of a threshold and decoys. Execution demoted two of the authors'
own rules (SC006, SC103) after their witnesses produced a repeated call the
provider absorbed rather than a second charge; both were downgraded rather than
quietly dropped. The prevalence sweep locates a threshold and explicitly does
not estimate how common it is in deployed pipelines.
